# What Is a Bear?

Let's revisit the theater metaphor in `what.md`. It seems a bit overwrought; I'd like to simplify. Here's my first draft:

LLMs are very good at writing the next line in a script, "exquisite corpse" style.

We can start a script for a play with one character, to be performed by a rather adept bear.

One step at a time, the writer sends instructions to our bear: often "say this out loud to the audience" but also "use this prop".

The bear does as they are told. Sometimes, the instruction is "think this". The bear knows they don't need to do anything, then; it's really just the writer's way of evolving the character between actions.

A stage manager watches, and adds notes to the script. "The audience answered this way" or "the prop make a noise". They then send the entire script to the writer.

The writer doesn't remember anything, but every turn it reads the entire script, with notes, and adds another instruction for the bear.

Some of the props are very complex. They can be notebooks that allow the character to remember things between plays, or calculators that let the writer get math done without mistakes.

Bear Den makes all of this happen with templates for scripts, some really useful props, and stages on which your bear can perform for you.
